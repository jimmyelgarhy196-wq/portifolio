You can lose money. The value of listed securities can fall as well as rise, and you may get back less than you invested — including nothing at all.

1. General market risk

Share prices move for reasons that no analysis can fully anticipate: company performance, sector conditions, macroeconomic changes, policy decisions, sentiment, and events with no warning. A well-researched position can still lose money.

2. Risks specific to the Egyptian market

  • Currency. The Egyptian pound has experienced significant devaluations. Returns measured in EGP may look very different measured in another currency.
  • Inflation and interest rates. High and variable inflation and policy rates affect both company earnings and the discount rate applied to them. A valuation model is highly sensitive to these inputs.
  • Liquidity. Many EGX-listed companies trade thinly. You may not be able to buy or sell the quantity you want at the price you see, and a quoted price may not be achievable.
  • Concentration. A small number of large constituents can dominate index moves, so an index level may not describe the typical stock.
  • Disclosure and reporting. Reporting timeliness and detail vary between issuers. Some data is simply not available, and where that is the case we say so.
  • Trading halts and regulatory action. Trading in a security can be suspended, sometimes for extended periods, during which you cannot exit a position.

3. Risks of the analysis itself

  • Model risk. A discounted cash flow model rests on assumptions about growth, margins and the discount rate. Small changes in those assumptions produce very different values. We publish a sensitivity grid for exactly this reason.
  • Data risk. Analytics are only as good as the data behind them. Where data is missing, a score is withheld or its coverage is reduced, and we state this.
  • Backward-looking indicators. Technical indicators describe past price behaviour. They do not predict future prices.
  • AI limitations. AI-assisted commentary can be incomplete or wrong even when constrained to available data.

4. Leverage and margin

{{ brand.company }} does not offer leverage, margin or any credit facility. If you use borrowed money to invest through a broker, your losses can exceed the amount you invested. That risk is between you and your broker.

5. Diversification is not protection

Spreading investments reduces some risks but does not eliminate them. In a broad market decline, diversified portfolios fall too.

6. Before you invest

  • Understand what the company does and how it makes money.
  • Read the company's own financial statements and EGX disclosures.
  • Decide in advance what would tell you the thesis was wrong.
  • Consider independent professional advice.
  • Only invest money you can afford to lose entirely.
