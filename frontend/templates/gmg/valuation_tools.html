How the DCF works

A two-stage model. Free cash flow grows from your starting rate, fading linearly to the terminal rate across the forecast years. Each year is discounted at the discount rate, and a Gordon terminal value is added and discounted back. Net debt is subtracted to reach equity value, which is divided by the share count.

The model refuses to produce a number when free cash flow is missing or negative, when the share count is unknown, or when terminal growth is not below the discount rate — cases where the arithmetic would be meaningless.

Egyptian market inputs

The default discount rate uses CAPM with an EGP risk-free rate, an equity risk premium and a beta — all editable. EGP rates have been high, which pushes discount rates well above what a dollar-based model would use and materially lowers present values.

Because a DCF is so sensitive to these two inputs, every model is published with a sensitivity grid across both.
